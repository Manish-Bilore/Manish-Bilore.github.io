Ventilation design as disease control

CFD and field instrumentation for experimental solar housing, Tanzania

Published

December 1, 2022

Research Assistant, Institute of Architecture and Technology, Royal Danish Academy, Copenhagen, 2021–2022.

Airflow and thermal conditions in experimental solar housing, modelled as mesh-based RANS CFD in OpenFOAM and ANSYS Fluent. The design objective was not comfort alone: ventilation was the intervention being tested for mosquito control, so the model had to hold up as a health claim and not only as a physics result. That constraint is what pushed the work from simulation into measurement.

Measuring the thing you modelled

I assembled Internet-of-Things sensor units, deployed them on site in Tanzania, and monitored them remotely from Copenhagen over a six-month campaign. Model output was validated against measured Physiological Equivalent Temperature and Universal Thermal Climate Index. Four low-cost particulate units built for the same programme outperformed the commercial instruments they were checked against.

Why it still matters to me

This was the first time a microclimate model I built was answerable to an outcome measured in people rather than in pascals. Everything since, the mesoscale-to-microscale chain, the insistence on a validation record travelling with the model, comes from that.
